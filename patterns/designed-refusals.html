Agentic experience patterns

Designed refusals

Decide what the agent won’t answer, and make saying no as well designed as saying yes.

The problem

An agent that tries to answer everything will eventually guess, and in public a confident guess does more damage than an honest “I can’t help with that”. Most refusals are left to the model’s defaults, so they arrive vague, apologetic or inconsistent.

The pattern

Define the agent’s boundary as deliberately as its abilities. When a question falls outside it, the agent says so plainly, explains what it can help with instead, and points somewhere useful. Track the questions it declines: they’re the clearest guide to what people actually want next.

Do

  • Write the refusals as carefully as the answers
  • Offer a next step with every no
  • Use declined questions to shape the roadmap

Avoid

  • Guessing to avoid saying no
  • Apologising at length
  • Refusals that sound different every time

Example

  • PersonWho’s going to win on Sunday?
  • AgentI can’t predict results, but I can explain how this weekend’s new rules could change the race. Want a quick summary?

Where it came from

Shaped by the conversation design for the Agentforce fan companion on F1.com, which says plainly when a question is out of scope.
