Agentic experience patterns

Read back what commits

If an action spends money, places an order or changes a record, the agent says it back and waits.

The problem

Speed is the point of an agent, but some mistakes can’t be undone. When a person is busy or the request was ambiguous, an agent that acts immediately turns a small misunderstanding into a wrong order, a wrong payment or a wrong record.

The pattern

Separate actions that commit from actions that don’t. For anything that commits, the agent restates exactly what it’s about to do, in plain language, and asks for a clear yes. Everything else stays fast.

Do

  • Restate the specific items, quantities or changes, not a summary
  • Ask a question that can be answered with a simple yes or no
  • Make cancelling as easy as confirming

Avoid

  • Confirming every trivial step, which trains people to say yes without listening
  • Vague confirmations such as “Shall I go ahead?”
  • Hiding the read-back in a wall of other information

Example

  • PersonReorder what they had last time.
  • AgentThat’s three products, the same quantities as last time. Shall I place it?
  • PersonYes.
  • AgentDone. It’s on your screen.

Where it came from

Developed on a voice agent for field sales reps at a FTSE 100 drinks bottler.
