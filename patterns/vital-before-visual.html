Agentic experience patterns

Vital before visual

Speak only what the person needs right now. Everything else waits for a screen.

The problem

Voice agents are usually designed as if the user is sitting at a desk with nothing else to do. Real people are driving between appointments, walking into a building or carrying something. Their ears are the most limited channel the agent has, and every extra word is attention taken from the task in front of them.

The pattern

Before writing any dialogue, sort every piece of information into four tiers: vital, important, helpful and hide. Only vital information earns a place in speech. Important and helpful information is shown on a screen when the person can look at one. Anything in the hide tier doesn’t appear at all.

Do

  • Sort information into tiers before you script a single line
  • Send prices, quantities and anything someone might compare to the screen
  • Tell the person where the rest is waiting

Avoid

  • Reading out everything the system knows
  • Treating the screen as a copy of what was said
  • Making people remember numbers they only heard once

Example

  • PersonWhat’s next?
  • AgentYour next visit is twelve minutes away. I’ve put their prices and last order on your screen for when you park.

An illustrative example.

Where it came from

Developed on a voice and multimodal agent for field sales reps at a FTSE 100 drinks bottler, across in-car, earbuds and in-app chat.
